Deploy the job-search proxy to your own Cloudflare account, then add its URL in Settings. Some job boards also require their own credentials. Availability and provider limits vary.

01 / RESUMESTailor the story, not the facts

A strong resume makes relevant experience easy to find. Keep a master copy, then create a version for each role.

  1. Read the description and identify three core requirements. Match each to a real project, responsibility, or accomplishment.
  2. Move the most relevant evidence higher. Write bullets as action + context + result; use numbers only when you can support them.
  3. Use the employer's terminology where it honestly fits. Never add tools, credentials, or experience you don't have.
  4. Use clear headings and a simple layout. Check the exported file for clipped text, working links, and correct contact details.

Try this: Rewrite one bullet to explain what you did and why it mattered. Review AI edits for accuracy; a score cannot guarantee an interview.

02 / YOUR ROUTINEBuild a search you can sustain

Plan around your actual time and energy, not someone else's application count.

  1. Write down your target roles, location needs, and non-negotiables. Use them to decide which openings deserve a closer look.
  2. Choose a manageable weekly rhythm: one session to shortlist, one to tailor applications, and one to follow up.
  3. Keep a simple record of the role, link, application date, contact, and next action. Confirm the listing on the employer's site.
  4. Review your process weekly. Adjust one thing, schedule breaks, and set a stopping time for each session.

Try this: Choose one suitable opening and put your next action on the calendar. Measure what you can control, not just replies.

03 / INTERVIEWINGPrepare to have a real conversation

You don't need a perfect script. You need clear examples and room to listen.

  1. Research the team's work and the role. Prepare two specific questions about priorities, expectations, or ways of working.
  2. Choose four real stories: a challenge, a collaboration, an achievement, and something you learned. Outline the situation, task, action, and result.
  3. Practice aloud. Be clear about your own contribution, keep the context brief, and invite follow-up questions.
  4. Check logistics and ask about any accommodations you need. Afterward, note what you learned and send a short, specific thank-you.

Try this: Practice a two-minute story with a friend. Ask which parts were clear and where they wanted more detail.

04 / NETWORKINGStart with curiosity, not a pitch

Useful connections can begin with a small, respectful conversation.

  1. Start with former colleagues, classmates, or people whose work genuinely interests you. Read their public work before reaching out.
  2. Write a brief introduction, explain what caught your attention, and ask one focused question. Make it easy to decline.
  3. If they agree to chat, respect the time offered. Ask about their experience rather than opening with a referral request.
  4. Thank them and share how their advice helped. If there's no reply, one polite follow-up is enough; then give them space.

Try this: “Hi [name], your work on [specific topic] caught my eye. I'm exploring [area]. Would you be open to sharing one thing you wish you'd known starting out? No worries if now isn't a good time.”
